\begin{abstract}


Multi-objective Bayesian optimization is increasingly used to accelerate materials discovery, yet standard frameworks neglect a critical practical constraint: many candidate designs are infeasible and yield no measurable objective values, wasting experimental resources. This work presents a risk-aware multi-objective Bayesian optimization framework that incorporates a learned feasibility classifier to probabilistically penalize candidates likely to produce failed experiments, while preserving exploration of uncertain but promising regions. The framework couples physics-informed Gaussian process surrogates with multi-fidelity property models and is demonstrated through high-throughput discovery of single-phase FCC high-entropy alloys in the Al--V--Cr--Mn--Fe--Co--Ni--Cu system for ballistic applications. From a feasible space of approximately 27{,}000 CALPHAD-screened compositions, 48 alloys were synthesized and characterized over three closed-loop iterations, targeting five objectives: yield strength, UTS/YS ratio, strain at UTS, dynamic-to-quasi-static strength ratio, and simulated depth of penetration. The risk-aware strategy expanded the Pareto front from 10 to 21 non-dominated alloys while increasing the hypervolume by approximately 30\% per iteration, efficiently navigating a high-dimensional, constraint-rich design space while exploring less than 0.2\% of the feasible compositions.


\end{abstract}
